# Personas

Proto-personas: built from assumptions and public information, to be checked against real interviews (see [the brief](./BRIEF.md#assumptions-to-validate)). Names and details are fictional.

---

## Dona Lúcia · the maker

**62 · ceramist · Cunha, São Paulo**

Dona Lúcia has shaped clay for thirty years. Her bowls and moringas are fired in a wood kiln behind her house. Her grandson set up an Instagram account for her, and orders now come from all over the country, but most of her evenings go into answering messages and writing addresses on paper.

> "I like talking to people who buy my pieces. I don't like losing an order because I forgot to answer."

**Goals**

- Show her work with the care it deserves, and tell the story of each piece.
- Know at a glance what was paid and what still needs to ship.
- Spend less time on the phone and more time at the wheel.

**Frustrations**

- Long forms and technical words.
- Small text and buttons that are hard to tap.
- Not knowing if a buyer actually paid.

**Context**

- Uses a mid-range Android phone, often with weak signal.
- Comfortable with WhatsApp and Instagram, unsure about "systems".
- Larger font size turned on in her phone settings.

**What this means for Moringa**

- A guided, step-by-step form with a preview before publishing.
- Plain words: "Pedidos para enviar", not "Fulfillment queue".
- Large touch targets, text that survives 200% zoom, and pages that load on 3G.
- A clear, single list of what to ship today.

## Marina · the buyer

**29 · UX writer · Recife, Pernambuco**

Marina likes objects with a story. Half of her apartment came from fairs and small shops she found online. She buys on her phone, usually at night, and she wants to know who made what she is buying.

> "When I buy something handmade, I'm also buying a little of someone's day. I want to know who that someone is."

**Goals**

- Find pieces by region and material, not just by price.
- Pay quickly, with no surprises.
- Know where her order is without asking the maker.

**Frustrations**

- Checkouts that ask for an account before she even sees the total.
- Orders that go silent after payment.
- Product pages with one photo and no details.

**Context**

- iPhone, often in dark mode.
- Saves things to "buy later" and comes back days after.
- Sometimes uses the browser in English.

**What this means for Moringa**

- Filters by state, material and price; favorites that survive sign-in.
- The total and the shipping rule visible before checkout.
- An order timeline with tracking, and a gentle nudge to review once it arrives.
- Dark theme and an English version that feel first-class.

## Accessibility lens (applies to both)

Some of the people who will use Moringa navigate with a keyboard, a screen reader or Windows High Contrast, or have low vision or tremors. Every flow is designed and tested for them too, following the same process used in the Ipê Design System.
